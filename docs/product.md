# Product spec

A wireless, low-profile mechanical keyboard with a built-in touchpad and a Unix layout. It works as a daily desk keyboard and as a couch keyboard for a media center.

## Glossary of technical terms, and terms to avoid

None yet.

## User facing feature and UX

- Unix layout in the style of the Happy Hacking Keyboard (HHKB): Control left of A, Backspace above Return, Backslash and backtick at the right end of the number row, and no function row.
- Bottom row of Alt, Super, Space, Super and Alt, as on the HHKB.
- A Fn key right of the right Shift. Fn gives function keys and media keys.
- Tapping Control sends Esc. Holding Control works as Control.
- The Esc key sends a left mouse click while a finger is on the touchpad and for a moment after it lifts. Otherwise it sends Esc.
- A touchpad right of the keys, as on the Logitech K400 Plus and the Framework Wireless Touchpad Keyboard.
- Pressing the touchpad clicks, with a haptic click felt through the pad, like an Apple trackpad.
- A two-finger tap on the touchpad sends a right mouse click.
- Full-size arrow keys in an inverted T below the touchpad.
- Kailh Choc low-profile switches.
- Multicolor 3D-printed keycaps with printed legends.
- A thin, portable profile like the Apple Magic Keyboard, with the dense, solid feel of a mechanical keyboard.
- The chassis is two identical 1.6 mm PCBs soldered together through every switch pin into a 3.2 mm slab. Switches, touchpad, controller and battery sit on top, soldered directly.
- The bottom is flat, with no switch pins poking through.
- Wireless, running from a rechargeable battery charged over USB-C.
- Pairs with more than one host, such as a desk computer and a media center, and switches between them.

## Non user facing features to make the device work

- Connects to hosts over Bluetooth.
- Neither PCB has copper around the controller's antenna, so the chassis does not block Bluetooth.
